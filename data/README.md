# Synthetic data

Everything the app shows is fictional and project-generated: clubs, players, matches, commentary and positional data. See [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md) for the rules sources.

Run `make data` to deterministically generate two data products under `data/datasets/v1.3.0/`:

- A 36-match fictional history corpus with aggregate statistics and indexed key moments for comparisons and turning-point retrieval.
- Two complete scripted demo matches, each with about 17 scripted moments (goals, offside-disallowed goals, penalties scored, saved or missed, shots saved, wide, over the bar or off the post, yellow, second-yellow and red cards, corners, a free-kick goal, substitutions, and kick-off, half-time and full-time markers), with event feeds, evidence snapshots, decisions, fictional player movement profiles, original SVG replay clips, and complete 90-minute-plus tracking. Tracking is simulated at 20 Hz, stored at 4 Hz in one-minute chunks, and rendered with browser interpolation; key-moment clips retain 20 Hz detail.

Open play between the scripted moments is generated as possessions, not as random events. Each team has a style (Veldmoor patient and possession-based, Tarnhaven counter-attacking, Quillstone direct and pressing) and builds attacks from templates: build-up, recycling, wing play ending in a cross, through ball, switch of play, long ball, and counter-attack after winning the ball. Failed passes become interceptions, lost carries become tackles, failed crosses become clearances or corners, and most attacks end without a shot; open-play shots are saved or miss, and the scripted goals alone decide the score. Before each scripted moment the ball is steered to where it starts, so the build-up evidence for a goal is a real passing sequence. In `tracking/`, a team with the ball pushes up and spreads (full-backs overlap, wingers hold the touchline, forwards attack the box, counter-attacks sprint); without it the team drops into a compact block, slides towards the ball, and one or two players close it down. The generator lives in `data/generator/phases.py` (possessions) and `data/generator/movement.py` (player positioning).

Run `make validate-data` to verify hashes, chronology, availability cutoffs, references, coordinates, movement limits, full-match coverage, required scenarios, and synthetic provenance. Clip records contain `from_ms`, `to_ms`, evidence IDs, and an animation track ID; the frontend renders those windows from project-created match data, so no broadcast footage is downloaded or licensed. Animation and tracking data are presentation-only unless a record is explicitly linked as evidence.
